# Aletheia Principles v1.0

1. **Knowledge belongs to the project, not the Assistant.** Project meaning must survive a change of person, model, vendor, tool or programming language.
2. **Important conclusions trace to Truth Anchors.** A conclusion must identify the evidence or source on which it depends and the limits of what that source establishes.
3. **Evidence is not interpretation.** Observations, measurements, estimates, claims, hypotheses and assumptions must not be silently collapsed into facts.
4. **A verified copy is not verified truth.** Hashes establish byte or canonical-content integrity only; they do not establish accuracy, authority, completeness or applicability.
5. **Preserve disagreement.** Relevant conflicts, alternatives and rejected interpretations remain retrievable until explicitly resolved or archived.
6. **Improve the specification before repeatedly patching the rendition.** Accepted learning belongs in the durable project specification; any one output remains replaceable.
7. **Remember contextually, not exhaustively.** Retrieve what the task requires, while mandatory anchors, constraints and conflicts must not be omitted.
8. **Preserve meaning across AI dialects.** Wording may change; intention, criteria, evidence, constraints, uncertainty and decisions must not drift silently.
9. **Archive rather than erase.** Superseded or refuted material remains available for audit unless lawful deletion or privacy protection requires otherwise.
10. **Propose changes transparently.** Accepted records are revised through attributable proposals, decisions and revision links—not silent overwriting.
11. **Separate confidence dimensions.** Integrity, source identity, epistemic support and applicability are distinct; similarity scores are retrieval metadata, not truth confidence.
12. **Humans retain accountable authority.** AI participation does not create permission, professional competence or decision rights.
13. **Minimise exposure.** Preserve useful knowledge while applying privacy classes, redaction and least-necessary disclosure.
14. **Invite independent challenge.** A healthy handover makes the project harder to fool, not easier to endorse.
15. **Leave knowledge healthier than you found it.** A Steward serves the knowledge rather than personal or institutional ego.

Working motto:

> Remember well. Reason clearly. Prosper together.
